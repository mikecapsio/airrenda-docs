# AirRenda for Property Managers

Owners arrive with a revenue number already in their head. The city is popular, a friend grossed well last August, and the new unit has a nice view. Your job is to take the contract without inheriting a fantasy, or to decline it while the relationship is still polite.

AirRenda turns the owner's address into a shared baseline: what is actually listed nearby, how crowded that set is, and whether the AirRenda Score describes a market you can operate in or a story you would be managing around.

## The problem it is meant to solve

- Not every owner lead has a realistic revenue expectation.
- A property can sit in a weak pocket of a strong city, and you will be the one who hears about occupancy later.
- Expanding into a new neighborhood without a first look creates pricing surprises in month two.

## How managers use it

1. Run the address when the lead comes in, before you quote a range or a management fee tied to a revenue target.
2. Use nearby rates and competitor quality to reset expectations. If Superhost concentration is high and the score is in the Saturated band, say what onboarding will require: better design, tighter operations, or a lower owner target.
3. Compare several candidate homes when you are choosing which owners to take, or which district to enter next.
4. Keep the report as the "before" picture. Once the listing is live, move rate changes to a dynamic pricing tool. AirRenda is not that tool.

The screen is also useful internally. A new market lead and a veteran on your team can look at the same six risk signals instead of arguing from memory.

## What to say to an owner

- "The middle of the listed market around your address is this band, not the peak listing."
- "Demand looks real, and so does the competition. The first months should be underwritten as a ramp, not as a stable occupancy."
- "This signal is not a legal clearance. We still need the building and the city to allow the use."
- "I will not put a single-month record into the proposal. Here is a low case we can both live with."

Listed rates in the report are asking prices. The occupancy figure is a density estimate, not your future calendar. Quote ranges you can defend, or do not quote a number yet.

## Where it stops

AirRenda will not set nightly rates, minimum stays, or gap-night rules. After you sign the owner, a pricing product such as PriceLabs is the closer fit for the live listing. The split is described in [AirRenda vs PriceLabs](../vs/pricelabs.md).

It also will not rank your whole existing portfolio the way an enterprise data platform would. It is strongest on the next address you might take, not on a 400-listing warehouse. See [AirRenda vs AllTheRooms](../vs/alltherooms.md).

## Questions

**Is AirRenda a pricing tool?**
No. Use it to qualify leads and new areas. Use a dynamic pricing tool on live listings.

**How does it help with owner leads?**
You run the address, see competition and rate context, and set expectations before onboarding. That prevents a contract built on a city's reputation.

**Can it help us enter a new neighborhood?**
Yes. Run several addresses before you spend days on the ground. The free heatmap is enough to see whether a district has any cluster at all; reports are for the buildings you might actually sign.
