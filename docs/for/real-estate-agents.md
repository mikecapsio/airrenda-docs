# AirRenda for Real Estate Agents

Buyers ask whether a listing could work as an Airbnb long before anyone is ready to hire an analyst. If you answer from the neighborhood's reputation, you set a number the rest of the transaction will be judged against. If you refuse to discuss it at all, another agent will, badly.

AirRenda gives you a first-pass report on the listing address: nearby supply, listed rates, property-type mix, competition, and the AirRenda Score. You can talk about the market without pretending to be the client's investment advisor.

## The problem it is meant to solve

- The Airbnb question shows up early, often at the first showing.
- You need something more specific than "tourists love this area."
- A casual revenue guess becomes the client's expectation, and then your problem.

## How agents use it

1. Run the report before a showing when you know the buyer cares about short-term rental income.
2. Walk the score category and the map, not a single income figure. "Strong Opportunity, with a lot of entire homes already reviewed above 4.8" is a useful sentence. "You will make $6,000 a month" is not.
3. Point at Regulation Exposure and say, out loud, that it is not a legal opinion. Offer to introduce local counsel or the building manager. Do not clear the use yourself.
4. Send serious buyers into real diligence: rules, costs, financing, inspection. The report qualifies the conversation. It does not close the investment question.
5. For two listings the buyer cannot decide between, run both so the comparison is the same shape.

The PDF is easy to leave behind. Label it as market research generated on a date. You are not issuing a projection.

## What good use sounds like

Say:

- Here is how many similar listings are already active around this address.
- Here is the middle of the listed nightly-rate range, not the highest ad on the block.
- The score sits in a competitive band, so a generic unit will have to earn its reviews against strong hosts.
- I cannot tell you this is allowed, or what you will net.

Do not say:

- The score means you should buy.
- The revenue range is what the client will earn.
- A low Regulation Exposure signal means the city will not care.

## Plans

Agents with the occasional STR-curious buyer can stay on a smaller plan. Agents who attach a report to a meaningful share of showings will burn through 10 reports quickly and should look at Investor volume. Current allowances are on https://airrenda.com/en/pricing. The report content does not change between paid plans.

Priority support is on the Agency plan, which is the closer fit if several agents share a heavy client flow. Enterprise is the conversation when you need invoicing and onboarding rather than a self-serve subscription.

## Questions

**Can I share reports with clients?**
Yes, as market research, with a clear statement that the report is not legal, tax, lending, or investment advice.

**Does AirRenda check the local rules?**
It flags a regulation-exposure pattern. You and the buyer still verify city law, permits, taxes, and building or HOA policy.

**Why not quote a city report I already have?**
The buyer is purchasing one address. Nearby competition changes by block. A city average cannot see that.
