# AirRenda for Acquisition Analysts

Acquisition teams lose hours when every teaser enters underwriting at the same depth. Some addresses deserve a memo. Others should stop after a market check that takes less time than building the tab.

AirRenda is that first gate. Every address gets the same screen: score band, listing density, listed-rate context, property-type mix, host-quality signals, and six risk signals. The output is comparable across a pipeline, which ad-hoc screenshots are not.

## The problem it is meant to solve

- Deal flow is wider than the time available for full models.
- Manual comp checks are hard to compare from one analyst to the next.
- Weak short-term rental markets still reach investment committee because the teaser was well written.

## How analysts use it

1. Make an AirRenda report the entry ticket for any deal whose thesis depends on nightly rental income.
2. Rank by category, then by the weakest factor and the loudest risk signal. Do not rank by the mid revenue case.
3. Escalate Strong Opportunity addresses into the model, with the report's low rate band as a required case.
4. Send Competitive and Saturated addresses forward only with a written differentiation note: why this asset beats the reviewed hosts already on the block.
5. Drop or reprice Unproven addresses unless demand is documented somewhere other than the teaser.
6. Refresh anything still in committee when the data is more than a few weeks old. One refresh per saved address each calendar month is included on paid plans and does not use a report.

Paste the score category, listing count, rate band, and Regulation Exposure into the market section of the memo. Then do the work the report cannot do: basis, capex, debt, tax, ramp-up, and downside.

## What belongs in the memo, and what does not

Belongs:

- "Nearby listed rates cluster between X and Y. We are not underwriting the maximum."
- "Score 58, Strong Opportunity. Competition Balance is the soft factor because Superhost share is high."
- "Regulation Exposure is elevated. Counsel is checking the ordinance and the building. No offer without that."

Does not belong:

- "AirRenda projects $N of NOI." It does not. The revenue range ignores your cost stack, and occupancy is not measured bookings.
- "Score 88, therefore highest priority." That band is saturation.
- "The tool cleared regulatory risk." It cannot.

## Volume

A weekly pipeline fits Investor (30 reports a month) only if many deals are recycled addresses. New addresses spend a credit each. Agency (70) is the self-serve step up, with priority support. Funds that need invoicing, a shared onboarding, and a custom allowance should use the Enterprise path on the pricing page rather than stretching a single seat.

Confirm current numbers at https://airrenda.com/en/pricing.

## Questions

**Can this support an investment committee memo?**
Yes, as the market-screen section. It does not replace the model, the regulation memo, capex, tax, or the operator's view.

**Why insist on the same tool for every deal?**
So a loud teaser cannot outrank a quiet one that has better local evidence. Consistency is the point.

**What should it stop us from doing?**
Building a full model for an address with almost no nearby listings, a rate band that misses the return hurdle on day one, or obvious saturation you have no plan to beat.
