# Getting Started with AirRenda

AirRenda is built for the moment you have a place in mind and need a straight read on the Airbnb market around it. This guide covers the first session: the free map, the first paid report, and how to use a shortlist without fooling yourself.

The live product is at https://airrenda.com/en. An annotated sample of the paid report is at https://airrenda.com/en/example-report.

## What you need

- A location. That can be a full street address or a click on the globe. Apartments, houses, condos, villas, studios, and townhomes all use the same flow, because the report describes the surrounding market, not the condition of the unit.
- A free account with Google sign-in before you generate address reports. You must be at least 18.
- A paid plan (Buyer, Investor, or Agency) for full reports. The Explorer heatmap does not require an account or a card.

You do not upload photos, a rent roll, or a 3D model.

## Step 1: Look at the map before you spend a report

Open the product and browse the interactive heatmap. Explorer is free. It shows where short-term rental listings cluster so you can see which districts have an active market before you pick a building.

The heatmap does not include the AirRenda Score, nightly-rate detail, risk signals, revenue ranges, or PDF downloads. Use it to narrow a city. Use a report when you have an address worth a credit.

## Step 2: Create an account and choose a plan

Sign in with Google. Then pick a plan on https://airrenda.com/en/pricing.

- Buyer is for a small shortlist: 10 new address reports per month.
- Investor is the plan marked most popular: 30 reports per month.
- Agency is for teams with heavier deal flow: 70 reports per month, plus priority support.
- Enterprise is custom. Use https://airrenda.com/en/contact.

Every paid plan includes the same report. You are buying volume. Current prices belong on the pricing page; do not rely on a copied number in an old note if the page has changed.

Each new address uses one report. Unused monthly reports do not roll over. If a burst of deals exceeds the allowance, subscribers can buy a top-up pack (5, 15, 30, 60, or 100 reports).

## Step 3: Run the first report

You can start in either of these ways:

1. Click a building, street, or point on the globe. AirRenda resolves that point to a location.
2. Type an address in the search bar and pick a suggestion.

Most reports finish in under 10 seconds. Some locations take longer when data is thin or slow. When the report is ready it is saved to your dashboard automatically.

Open the example report first if you want to learn the layout before spending a credit on a live address.

## Step 4: Read it in this order

A report is easy to misuse if you start at the revenue number. Use this order:

1. **Market category and AirRenda Score.** Note the band, not just the digits. See [How the AirRenda Score works](how-the-airrenda-score-works.md).
2. **Active listing count.** Too few listings means the market is unproven. A very dense cluster can mean demand, saturation, or both.
3. **Nightly-rate range.** Look at the median and the P25–P75 band, not only the maximum. These are listed asking prices of nearby listings, not confirmed booked rates.
4. **Property-type mix.** Entire homes and private rooms do not support the same thesis. Check whether your property matches what is actually renting nearby.
5. **The six risk signals.** Especially Competition Intensity, Pricing Pressure, and Regulation Exposure.
6. **Revenue range last.** Low, mid, and high are scenarios built from those listed rates and an estimated occupancy rate. Occupancy is not measured from bookings. See [How to read a report](how-to-read-a-report.md).

Then decide: deeper diligence, a softer offer, or a pass. The report does not make that decision for you.

## Step 5: Compare, export, and refresh

Saved reports can be filtered by city and country. Run the same analysis on every address in a shortlist so the comparison is fair.

Download the PDF when you need to share the screen with a partner, a client, or a file for later. If you share it, say plainly that it is market research, not legal, tax, lending, or investment advice.

When a property stays on the list, refresh that saved address. Paid plans include one refresh per saved address each calendar month, and a refresh does not use another report. Run a fresh read close to an offer. Markets move.

Check Prices Around lets you browse live nearby listings on the map. That search uses one report from your balance. Use it when you need the pins, not as a substitute for the scored report.

## A short screen for a real shortlist

Use this when you have more addresses than afternoons:

1. Drop anything that is only a city story ("Barcelona does well") until you have a street address.
2. Run AirRenda on each address.
3. Set aside Unproven markets unless you have another reason to believe demand exists.
4. Treat Saturated (above 80) as a warning, not a trophy. You need a real way to stand out, or a price that assumes pressure.
5. Prefer addresses in the 41–65 band when you are comparing otherwise similar deals, then read the risk signals before you celebrate the score.
6. Check that nightly rates in the middle of the range, not the peak listing, can support your costs.
7. Note Regulation Exposure. Then verify the actual rules locally. The signal is not a permit.
8. Only the addresses that survive go into a spreadsheet with financing, furnishing, cleaning, management, taxes, and a downside case.

The blog walks through the same idea in more detail: https://airrenda.com/en/blog/airbnb-deal-screening-checklist

## What to do after a report looks good

AirRenda stops at the market screen. Before money moves, confirm at least:

- Whether short-term rental use is allowed for that unit, building, and city.
- HOA, lease, and permit limits.
- Taxes and platform fees.
- A cost model that still works if rates and occupancy come in at the low end of the report.
- Inspection, insurance, and financing terms.
- How long a new listing may take to earn reviews if nearby hosts are already strong.

## If the report looks thin

A low score or a small listing count is information. It usually means there is not enough nearby Airbnb activity to support an aggressive revenue story. You can still buy the property for other reasons. You should not underwrite it as if it sat in a proven short-term rental pocket.

Smaller and emerging markets often look like this. That is the product working, not a failed search.
