# How to Read an AirRenda Report

An AirRenda report is a snapshot of the short-term rental market around one address at the time you generate it. Every paid plan includes the same sections. This page explains what each part is allowed to mean.

See a full sample at https://airrenda.com/en/example-report.

## The sections, in the order that keeps you honest

### 1. AirRenda Score and market category

The headline number is 0–100, with a category: Unproven, Early Opportunity, Strong Opportunity, Competitive, or Saturated. Read the category. A high number can be a warning.

The report also shows a four-factor breakdown: Demand Signal, Revenue Potential, Competition Balance, and Market Quality. Use it when two addresses have similar totals and you need to know why. Weights and bands are in [How the AirRenda Score works](how-the-airrenda-score-works.md).

### 2. Active listings and the map

The listing count is the number of nearby short-term rentals the analysis found. The map shows how they cluster around the address.

Use the count as evidence, not as a vibe:

- A handful of listings can mean a quiet market or a new one. Do not call it proven demand.
- A deep cluster can mean guests already stay here. It can also mean you would be one more similar listing.
- Pins show proximity. A city can be famous and the block in front of you can still be empty, or packed.

Check Prices Around is separate from the scored report. It is a live map browse of nearby listings, and it uses one report from your balance.

### 3. Nightly rates

You should see average, median, minimum, maximum, and an interquartile band (P25–P75).

These are **listed asking prices** of nearby listings. They are not the rates guests actually paid, and they are not a booked-night ADR series. Hosts list optimistically. Discounts, gaps, and seasonality sit outside a single asking price.

For underwriting, start from the median and the P25–P75 band. Treat the maximum as a ceiling somebody is advertising, not as your base case. If many listings are discounted, the Pricing Pressure signal is the place that shows up.

### 4. Property-type mix

The report splits the nearby market, including entire homes versus private rooms, and it surfaces typical size where the data supports it. Match this to the property you are actually buying.

An entire-home purchase in a market dominated by private rooms is a different bet. So is a studio surrounded by large villas. The score's Revenue Potential factor cares about this mix; you should too.

### 5. Host and guest-quality signals

Expect average rating, Superhost share, and guest-favorite share, plus short market narratives about guest profile, rental type, and host standards.

High ratings and a high Superhost share usually mean guests are being served well. They can also mean a new host will struggle to look competitive on day one. That tension is why Competition Intensity exists as its own signal. Strong operators nearby are not free proof that you will match them.

### 6. Six investment risk signals

Each signal is a plain-language rating. Read them as a set.

| Signal | Question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Demand Strength | Is guest demand actually showing up, through listings, reviews, and guest-favorite signals? |
| Revenue Quality | Do listed rates suggest real pricing power, or a budget market with a low ceiling? |
| Competition Intensity | Will you be up against experienced, highly rated hosts? |
| Operational Complexity | Does the mix of entire homes, price level, and density imply heavier management? |
| Regulation Exposure | Does this pocket look like the dense entire-home pattern that often draws short-term rental rules? |
| Pricing Pressure | Are listings widely discounted, which often means hosts are struggling to fill nights? |

Regulation Exposure is the one people over-read. A high rating means the pattern of supply resembles markets where cities later restrict short stays. It is not a statement that this address is illegal, and a low rating is not a permit. Check the city, the building, the HOA, and a local lawyer before you rely on nightly income.

### 7. Revenue range

The report shows a low, mid, and high monthly revenue estimate, and related occupancy and annualized figures where those fields are present.

How to say this accurately:

- The rate input is the nearby listed price context (median, with a wider band for the low and high cases).
- Occupancy is an **estimate based on how dense the nearby listing market is**. It is not counted from bookings, calendars, or payouts.
- Monthly figures scale that rate by the occupancy estimate. They are scenarios, not a forecast for your unit.
- Nothing in the range deducts cleaning, utilities, supplies, management, platform fees, taxes, insurance, mortgage, or vacancy beyond the occupancy assumption.
- A new listing rarely earns the "mid" case in month one. Nearby listings with hundreds of reviews had time you will not have on day one.

Use the low case as the first number you try to survive. If the deal only works on the high case, it is fragile.

AirRenda is not a historical revenue database. If you need booked occupancy, ADR, and RevPAR over time, that is a different class of tool. The comparison with AirDNA is in [AirRenda vs AirDNA](vs/airdna.md).

### 8. Top competitors

The report lists standout nearby listings so you can see what a guest would actually compare you with: property type, price position, and host signals. This is a comp starter, not a finished comp set.

A useful comp is similar in type, size, and guest experience, and close enough that the same demand reaches it. Drop trophy listings that do not resemble the property. The blog note on comp sets is https://airrenda.com/en/blog/how-to-build-airbnb-comps-set.

## What the report will not tell you

- Whether you may legally operate a short-term rental there.
- What you should pay for the building.
- What the unit will earn after your specific costs.
- How the market looked three years ago.
- What to charge on a given night once you are live. That is a pricing tool's job. See [AirRenda vs PriceLabs](vs/pricelabs.md).
- That the seller's pro forma is right.

Listings appear and disappear, platforms change what they show, and seasons move prices. For a live deal, refresh the saved address close to the offer. Paid plans include one refresh per saved address each calendar month, and it does not use an extra report.

## A one-page pass you can reuse

Write four lines before you open a spreadsheet:

1. Category and score, plus the weakest of the four factors.
2. Listing count and whether the property type matches the local mix.
3. Median listed rate and the P25–P75 band. One sentence on discount pressure.
4. The risk signal you would be embarrassed to ignore, especially regulation or competition.

If you cannot write those four lines, you do not have a market thesis yet. You have a number.

## Sharing a report

Agents and partners can use the PDF in a conversation. Say what it is: a market screen around the address, generated on a date, not advice to buy and not a promise of income. The person who will own or manage the property still has to verify rules and costs.
