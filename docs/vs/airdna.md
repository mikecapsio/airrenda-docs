# AirRenda vs AirDNA

AirDNA is one of the best-known names in short-term rental market intelligence. AirRenda is narrower on purpose. It is for the moment you have a real address and need to know whether the Airbnb market around it looks healthy, crowded, or too thin to underwrite.

Use both if your process has room for both. Use AirRenda when the decision is "does this building deserve the rest of the work?"

The site version of this comparison is https://airrenda.com/en/vs/airrenda-vs-airdna.

## Quick verdict

Choose **AirRenda** for a fast, investor-readable screen of a specific address or a shortlist.

Choose **AirDNA** when you need historical occupancy, ADR, revenue trends, and broader market benchmarking after an address has survived that screen.

## At a glance

| Need | AirRenda | AirDNA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary workflow | Address-level Airbnb screening for investors, buyers, agents, and analysts | Short-term rental market intelligence and historical performance |
| Speed | Built for a first pass on a live list of properties | Richer dashboards, which take longer to interpret |
| Decision output | Score, market category, nearby density, listed-rate context, competitors, risk signals | Occupancy, ADR, RevPAR-style metrics, trends, projections |
| Best buyer | Someone deciding whether an address earns a model, a viewing, or an offer | Operators and analysts who need a mature STR dataset |
| Revenue honesty | Listed asking prices plus a density-based occupancy estimate, shown as a range | Historical performance products are a different evidence class; confirm current AirDNA methodology on their site |

## Where AirRenda is the better fit

- You are comparing five apartments, houses, or villas and need the same read on each.
- A broker has already handed you a revenue number and you want the local market to challenge it.
- You want saturation and competition in the same view as demand, so a busy market is not mistaken for an easy one.
- You do not want to open a full market-intelligence project just to decide whether to book a viewing.

The AirRenda Score compresses that screen into bands. 41–65 is the usual sweet spot. Above 80 is a saturation warning, not a trophy. Details: [How the score works](../how-the-airrenda-score-works.md).

## Where AirDNA still wins

- You need history: how occupancy and rates moved, not only what is listed today.
- You are benchmarking a market, a submarket, or a portfolio rather than triaging one address.
- The check size justifies a deeper dataset before committee.

AirRenda does not claim to be that dataset. Its occupancy figure is not measured from bookings. If someone tells you the two products "both show occupancy," ask which one is counted from performance and which one is estimated from how many listings sit nearby.

## Using them together

1. Screen the address in AirRenda.
2. If the category is Unproven, or the rate band cannot support the basis even on paper, stop.
3. If the screen is supportive, take the address into AirDNA (or your own comp file) for historical depth.
4. Model costs yourself. Neither product is the purchase decision.

## Questions

**Is AirRenda an AirDNA alternative?**
For fast address-level screening, yes. It is not a replacement for every AirDNA workflow.

**Which should I open before an offer?**
AirRenda on the exact address first. If the signals hold, continue with underwriting, local rules, expenses, and any deeper data your process requires.

**Can a team use both?**
Yes. AirRenda at the top of the funnel, AirDNA when the remaining deals justify historical research.
