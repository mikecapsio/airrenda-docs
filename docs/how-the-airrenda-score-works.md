# How the AirRenda Score Works

The AirRenda Score is a 0–100 market screen for the short-term rental activity around one address. It is there to answer a narrow question: does the nearby Airbnb market look unproven, early, healthy, crowded, or saturated?

It is not a property grade, a cap rate, or a buy signal. Two buildings a few blocks apart can score differently. That is intentional. City averages hide those gaps.

The public methodology page is https://airrenda.com/en/methodology/airrenda-score. If this guide and that page ever diverge, use the live page.

## Read the band, not only the number

| Score | Category | What it usually means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–20 | Unproven Market | Very little nearby short-term rental activity. Do not treat demand as proven. |
| 21–40 | Early Opportunity | Some demand, limited competition. First-mover potential is possible, and so is a market that is simply quiet. |
| 41–65 | Strong Opportunity | Proven demand with competition that may still be manageable. This is the band many new investors should hope to see. |
| 66–80 | Competitive Market | Demand is real and the field is crowded. Differentiation, operations, and property quality matter more. |
| 81–100 | Saturated | Heavy supply and pricing pressure. More realistic for experienced operators than for a first Airbnb. |

A score of 90 is not "better" than a score of 55. It often means you would be entering a market that is already full. A score of 15 is not a hidden bargain by itself. It means the data does not yet show a guest market.

## What goes into the score

The score is a weighted blend of four factors. These weights are part of the public product description:

| Factor | Weight | What it is looking at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demand Signal | 30% | Whether guest demand looks real: nearby listing presence, review activity, and guest-favorite signals. |
| Revenue Potential | 25% | Whether the area shows pricing power, including rate levels, the share of entire homes, and typical size. |
| Competition Balance | 25% | Whether supply is enough to prove a market without tipping into oversupply. Host concentration and discounting pull this down when new entrants would be squeezed. |
| Market Quality | 20% | Whether ratings and host standards look like a market guests already trust. |

The score is a composite of those factors. A pretty rate level cannot fully offset a dead listing count, and a huge listing count is not allowed to masquerade as an easy win. Competition Balance is designed to peak when demand is proven and there is still room, and to fall when the pocket looks oversupplied.

AirRenda does not publish the internal point tables behind each factor. Use the bands and the factor labels in the report. Do not invent a formula from this guide.

## What the score deliberately leaves out

Regulation Exposure is one of the six risk signals on the report. It is not mixed into the 0–100 score. A location can look commercially active and still be a regulatory problem. Read the signal beside the score.

The score also does not include:

- Purchase price, renovation cost, or cash-on-cash return.
- Your financing rate.
- Whether the building, HOA, or city allows short stays.
- Seasonality beyond what current listings imply.
- The condition, design, or photo quality of the unit you might buy.
- Your ability to operate.

Those belong in underwriting after the screen.

## How to use it on a shortlist

1. Sort addresses into the five categories before you sort them by a single point.
2. Keep Strong Opportunity (41–65) at the front of the line unless a risk signal gives you a reason not to.
3. Open Competitive (66–80) only if you already know how this property will be different from the listings around it.
4. Treat Saturated (81–100) as a reason to demand a better purchase price or a sharper operating plan, not as proof of a great deal.
5. Treat Unproven (0–20) as "the Airbnb story is not supported here yet."
6. Read Early Opportunity (21–40) twice. Limited competition is only attractive when you can explain where the guests will come from.

Then open the four-factor breakdown. A mid-range score with a weak Revenue Potential factor tells a different story than the same score with weak Competition Balance.

## A good score still needs the rest of the report

Pair the score with:

- Listing count, so you can see whether "demand" is three listings or thirty.
- The nightly-rate band, so the revenue story is anchored to listed comps.
- Property-type mix, so you are not comparing a private room thesis to an entire-home market.
- Discount pressure and Superhost concentration, which show up inside competition and the risk signals.
- Regulation Exposure, separately.

The long version of that read is in [How to read a report](how-to-read-a-report.md).

## Questions people ask about the score

**What is a good AirRenda Score?**
For most new investors, 41–65. It is the band where demand looks real and competition may still be workable.

**Is higher always better?**
No. Very high scores often mean heavy competition, pricing pressure, and a market that is harder to enter.

**Can the score tell me to buy?**
No. It is an address-level market screen. It is not legal, tax, lending, appraisal, or investment advice.

**Why might two nearby addresses differ?**
Because the inputs are the listings around each point. Density, rates, and host mix can change over a short distance, especially in tourist cities.

**What if there are almost no listings?**
Expect a low score and an Unproven or Early category. That is a finding. Validate demand some other way before you underwrite short-term rental income.
